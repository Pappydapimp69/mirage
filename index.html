MIRAGE

Survey the basin with five companions. Every one of you carries a meter you cannot see, and it only counts down. At zero, a mind starts inventing the world — theirs, or yours.

Goal. Find and survey all six markers, then get back to camp with at least two companions still walking with you.

The meters are invisible. Nobody has a bar. You read your party instead: who lags, who goes quiet, who starts narrating a ridge that isn't moving. Check in on anyone (1–5) to ask how they are — but a fraying companion shades the truth, and one who is gone will tell you they're fine.

The first five minutes are calm. Nothing drains. After that the meter falls, constantly, for the rest of the basin — and nothing stops it for good.

Pylons fire once, and take two. Stand in one and press survey to set hands on it — then somebody else in the same light has to do the same before it gives. Everyone inside catches it, the decay is held off for ten seconds, and that pylon is dead for the rest of the run. It does not recharge.

That second pair of hands is the check. When you are not yourself you will be shown pylons that aren't there, and spent ones that look full. They prime like real ones. What they never do is get confirmed — because there is nothing there for anyone else to touch. Three lumen doses exist for six people.

When you go. The screen stops being a witness — markers appear where there are none, a sixth companion joins the formation, spent pylons glow like full ones, north moves. Logging one of those writes down something that was never there, and nothing on screen will say so.

The record is what you are graded on. Come home with entries naming markers that are not out there and the survey is thrown out — nobody can tell which half to trust. To keep an entry honest, check in with a companion standing with you before you log it: someone you asked will refuse a marker that isn't there. Standing near people is not the same as checking with them, and a companion who is gone will agree with anything.

Fixing it. Go back to where an entry claims a marker and survey again. Finding nothing is what strikes it from the record — but only if your own head is clear at the time, and you will not be told which of those you are.

Items turn up in the fog too — a flare, a tether, a lens, and the Husk: real, but useless. What the item bar SAYS an item is may not be what it truly is while you're gone (or, sometimes, it's exactly right — you can't tell which), and something you pick up while hallucinating might not be there at all. Either way it can be carried and used — you only find out which kind of surprise it was the moment you use it. In couch co-op, the shared pack shows a DIFFERENT label to each of you — whoever's still lucid sees the truth about the same slot.

Give. B (or the gamepad B) gives the selected item to a nearby companion. This is the only verb in the game that can tell you something about your own state: offer a lucid companion something that was never there and they will say so — the one way to find out that the last thing you picked up, or were handed, or built, was a lie. It is not a free probe, though. A companion only accepts something they could actually use, and if it IS real they use it on the spot. So a failed offer costs you the slot's worth of doubt, and a successful one costs you the item: you weren't testing a theory, you were trying to help somebody.

Carrying and dropping. Three slots. V (or D-pad Down) puts the selected one down at your feet — a real item stays there to be picked up again, and a phantom simply stops being. Dropping is how you make room without spending anything.

Crafting. Carrying two combinable items and pressing craft fuses them into something stronger — an Ember, a Beacon, or a Ward, each doing both of its parts' work at once. A small indicator names what's craftable the moment it's possible, so you never have to guess and press blind. Carrying three combinable items, the CYCLED slot picks which pair fuses — the hint always names what you'll actually get. Crafting reads what you're ACTUALLY carrying, not what the item bar told you, so a hallucinating lead can still reach for a pair that doesn't combine — or doesn't exist.

Trees and stone turn up too — walk up and HOLD survey/pick up to gather: chop wood, or mine stone. It takes a moment (watch the prompt fill), and letting go early or wandering to a different one starts it over. Neither a tree nor a deposit ever lies about what it is. Enough of both crafts a Stake — plant it anywhere and it works exactly like a pylon from then on.

Pause (Esc, or Start on a pad) stops the basin completely — the clock and every meter hold while you're in there, so it is a safe place to think, not a risk. Volume lives in that menu too.

The basin isn't alone. Clearing one survey pushes the party into the next, harder basin — carrying doses, items, materials, and every scar with them. The campaign ends when you fail one, or clear the last.

Couch co-op. Pick it on the title screen, then a second controller presses Start mid-run to take over a companion — split screen, and each of you hallucinates on your own. They can drop back out with Select; the party AI simply resumes that mind.
